Read the text

Extracts the printed details, then checks whatever machine-readable data the document carries: the ICAO 9303 check digits in a passport or ID strip, or the PDF417 barcode printed on the back of a US or Canadian driving licence.

Read this as a data test, not a verdict. Passing means the numbers on the page agree with each other; it does not prove the document is genuine, because anyone who can compute the check digits can also print them. Failing usually means the photo, not the document.

Privacy: the recognition engine is downloaded from a CDN the first time you press the button, and again on later uses unless your browser has cached it. Your photo is never uploaded — recognition runs in a local Web Worker inside your browser, and the extracted text stays on this page. Press Clear results when you are done. If you would rather not fetch anything, leave this button alone; everything else in the tool works offline.

None of this is a substitute for a verifier reading the physical document, but it tells you what a genuine one should look like.

Genuineness signals

Forensic indicators of whether this is a straight camera photo of a physical document — or whether it has been edited, spliced, re-photographed from a screen, or printed and rescanned. These are signals, not a verdict.

Read this before acting on any of the above. These are statistical signals about the image, not a judgement about your document. A poor photo of a perfectly genuine ID can raise flags here, and a careful forgery can raise none. Specifically: (1) security printing such as guilloche lines and micro-text is normal and is not treated as a defect — only patterns in the blank area around the document count as recapture; (2) a region pasted in and then re-saved as a JPEG usually leaves no measurable trace, so a clean result is not proof that nothing was edited; (3) an out-of-focus photo cannot be assessed for sensor noise at all. Real document verification works in ways a browser page cannot: reading the NFC chip in an e-passport or e-ID, validating the machine-readable zone against the issuing authority's format, inspecting holograms and UV features in person, and querying official databases. Use this to check that your own capture is clean — not to judge someone else's document.
